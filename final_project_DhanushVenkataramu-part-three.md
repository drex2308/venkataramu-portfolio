# The final data story
This is my final [website](https://carnegiemellon.shorthandstories.com/from-farms-to-tables/index.html) for my story "From Farms to Tables".


# Changes made since Part II
In the second part of my project, I received feedback from a few people about the visual design and overall impact of my website. I made some changes based on their input, and after reading a few more articles, I decided to make a few additional updates to improve it further.

### Real Life Testimonial :
I added a section featuring testimonials from key voices in agriculture. One is about a agricultural investor, and the other is a farmer who has achieved significant success through sustainable practices and critical thinking. The farmer shares how they innovated new techniques in agriculture, leading to substantial profits. These real life examples aim to inspire others and highlight the impact of forward-thinking approaches in the agricultural sector.

### Benefits of Sustainable Agricultural Practices:
I added a section highlighting the benefits of sustainable agriculture. A review of 60 studies indicates that adopting sustainable agricultural practices reduces both upfront investments and maintenance costs. This information underscores the financial and environmental advantages of sustainable methods in modern farming.

### Breaking Down Logistics for Investors and Farmers:
I transformed complex logistics data into easy-to-understand metrics using relatable comparisons like truckloads. This approach makes it clearer for both investors and farmers to visualize the scale of agricultural demand and identify opportunities to meet the rising need for various products.

### Comparing Visuals (Pie Charts) in Exports and Imports:
I added a section that uses pie charts to visually compare the proportions of exports and imports. These charts help illustrate the differences and trends in agricultural products moving in and out of the USA and India, making it easier for investors and farmers to grasp the market landscape at a glance. The data to create the visualization was not readily available, so I had to get bigger datasets from USDA and Indian ministry of Agriculture to filter specific columns and data sheets required to build this summarized visual to provide comparative information to the stakeholders.

### Tree-Map Illustration
Based on the in-class recommendations and reviwes I received post the presentation, to aide the intent of focusing on the highest product categories exported by India and availabilty of an opportunity across differnt categories in USA imports, I chose the Tree-Map Visualization. I recieved positive feedback on how my story gained more traction in this change, I showed both illustration during these interviews (tree-map first) and the audience liked the tree-map more, the justification being it added more context and clarity to the story than a pie chart


## The Audience
The audience for this project includes **Indian farmers**, and **agricultural investors**. The story highlights the growing demand for Indian agricultural products in the U.S. and showcases the potential for farmers and investors to capitalize on this opportunity. For general public, the goal is to raise awareness of the increasing presence of Indian foods in their local markets and why this trend benefits both economies.

Since the audience is diverse, ranging from industry professionals to general consumers, I made sure the content was simple yet informative. I focused on using straightforward language and clear visualizations to ensure that everyone, from farmers to investors and even casual consumers, can easily understand the story. From the start, I aimed to present the data in a way that’s both engaging and accessible to all, regardless of their background in agriculture or trade.

I also sought feedback from a range of people, some of whom were aware of the trends in U.S.-India trade, while others were not.

**Person 1**: He is an investor who has been following the agricultural market closely. He mentioned that he appreciated the clear presentation of the data, especially how the truckload metrics brought the scale of the imports to life. He absolutely loved the real life examples and wanted to research himself more on this.

I also shared the website with my parents, who are part involved in the agricultural sector and they intrigued by the story. They found the data to be compelling, and my dad, who has experience in numbers (accountant by profession), noted that the visualizations were engaging and effective in drawing attention to the key points and trends.

## Final design decisions
I have added more sections and kept the color coordinated for all these sections. For a tree map that I added, I chose to go with green color as it represents farm green and sustainability. I have kept color throughout the website very coordinated and subtle. I also added a tree map insted of pie chart to show an comparative visualization between USA exports and Indian imports, a key part of story about an upcoming opportunity.

# Final Thoughts

When I began this project, I spent a considerable amount of time on Part I, and I believe this was the most crucial and time-consuming stage. Choosing the right topic and carefully structuring how I wanted to present the information was key—especially in a project that revolves around telling a compelling story. I wanted to ensure that the narrative I built was both informative and impactful to the userbase I was focusing on, while keeping the general public engaged. This strong foundation made the following stages easier, as I could simply follow the plan, making small adjustments as needed.

In Part II, as I started implementing the structure I laid out, it became clear that visualization was going to be a critical component. The flow of the story mattered immensely, as I had to effectively communicate the key points: the problem of rising U.S. imports of Indian foods, the reasons behind this surge, and the opportunities this presents for Indian farmers and investors. I made sure to present this in a way that both investors and farmers could easily grasp. Using relatable examples and metrics, I aimed to help everyone—whether familiar with agricultural logistics or not—see the potential of this growing market.

Through the feedback I received from industry experts and individuals aware of the opportunities in agriculture, I gained deeper insights into how I could make the story even stronger. These insights helped shape Part III of the project, where I refined the message to resonate more clearly with the audience. Overall, this project taught me the importance of thoughtful planning and the power of storytelling in making complex information accessible and actionable.
